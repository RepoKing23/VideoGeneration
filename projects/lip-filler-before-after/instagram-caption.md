# Instagram caption — mini lip plump

Cleo's original, for reference:

> Before and after mini lip plump for a client getting her lips done. Used a
> conservative approach, start slow and go slow, as she wanted to be sure of the
> size and volume she wanted. Her results are natural, added a bit of volume and
> hydration and maintain the shape of her natural lips.

The strongest thing in there is the *restraint*. Most filler content shouts
about volume; "we deliberately did less, and she stayed in control" is the
angle almost nobody runs, and it's the one that books nervous first-timers.
All three options below lead with that.

Instagram only shows the first line or two before "…more", so each option front-loads the hook.

---

## Option A — the trust angle (recommended)

```
She didn't want new lips. She wanted hers, just a little more. 💋

Mini lip plump — done the slow way.

We started conservative on purpose. A small amount, placed carefully, so she
could watch the shape settle and decide for herself how much volume actually
felt like her.

You can always add. You can't un-add.

The result: a touch more volume, a lot more hydration, and the natural lip
shape she walked in with completely intact.

Going slow isn't being cautious. It's the technique. 🤍

Ready to start slow? Comment CONSULT or book via the link.
```

## Option B — short and punchy

```
Mini lip plump 💋

Not bigger. Just better.

She wanted to be sure before committing to volume, so we went slow — a
conservative start, a little hydration, and her natural shape kept exactly
as it was.

Small change. Big difference.

Consults open now 🔗
```

## Option C — engagement-led

```
This is 1ml of restraint. 👀

Mini lip plump, conservative approach. She wasn't sure how much volume she
wanted yet — so we didn't decide it for her. We started small, kept her natural
lip shape, and let hydration do a lot of the work.

Subtle is a skill.

First time considering filler? Comment SLOW and I'll walk you through how a
conservative build actually works. 👇
```

Option C's comment prompt is the one most likely to lift reach — Instagram
weights comments heavily, and "ask a nervous beginner to self-identify" is an
easy, low-commitment reply.

## Before posting

- **Consent.** This is an identifiable client's treatment imagery. Most regions
  expect written consent covering social use specifically, not just treatment.
- **Claims.** The wording above avoids promising a result or a duration, which
  is where cosmetic advertising rules usually bite. If your clinic has approved
  claim language, send it and I'll rework these around it.
- The reel is silent by design — pick a calm trending audio in-app, which
  generally travels further than a baked-in track.
